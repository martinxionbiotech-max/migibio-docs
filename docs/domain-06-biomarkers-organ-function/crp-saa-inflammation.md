# CRP & SAA: Inflammation Markers Guide

CRP and SAA are **acute-phase proteins** — the fastest-rising markers of systemic inflammation. CRP is the primary canine marker; SAA is the primary feline marker. Both are quantitative and both transform inflammatory-disease management.

## Species Specificity

| Marker | Species | Reference Range | Elevated |
|--------|---------|-----------------|----------|
| CRP | Canine | < 10 mg/L | > 10 mg/L — inflammation |
| SAA | Feline | < 5 µg/mL | > 5 µg/mL — inflammation |

> **Don't swap species**: CRP is the dominant canine APP; SAA is the dominant feline APP. Using the wrong marker reduces sensitivity.

## Why Acute-Phase Proteins Matter

Acute-phase proteins rise within **4–24 hours** of an inflammatory stimulus — far faster than clinical signs or leukogram changes. This makes them ideal for:

1. **Early detection** — catch inflammation before it is clinically obvious.
2. **Monitoring treatment** — a falling CRP/SAA confirms the treatment is working.
3. **Differentiating disease** — inflammatory vs non-inflammatory.

## The Monitoring Advantage

A dog with pancreatitis has CRP 85 mg/L → after 3 days of treatment → CRP 20 mg/L. The **trend** confirms improvement, even if the patient still "looks" sick. A qualitative test cannot do this.

## FAQ

**How fast do CRP/SAA rise?** Within 4–24 hours of an inflammatory stimulus.

**Can CRP/SAA diagnose a specific disease?** No — they indicate inflammation, not the cause. Use with other tests to localize the disease.

## Case Study: Monitoring the Pancreatitis Patient

A 7-year-old Schnauzer presents with vomiting and cranial abdominal pain. A presumptive diagnosis of pancreatitis is made.

- **CRP at admission: 85 mg/L** — severe systemic inflammation.
- **Treatment:** IV fluids, antiemetics, analgesia.
- **CRP at 48 h: 32 mg/L** — falling, confirming response.
- **CRP at 96 h: 12 mg/L** — near-normal.

**Lesson:** A qualitative test would have read "positive" every day. Quantitative CRP told the story — the *trend* confirmed the treatment was working before the patient visibly improved.
