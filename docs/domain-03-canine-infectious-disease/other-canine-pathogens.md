# CCV, CAV, and Other Canine Pathogen Testing

Beyond CPV and CDV, several other canine pathogens are tested in small-animal practice. This page covers the secondary menu: **CCV, CAV, Leishmania, and Toxoplasma**.

## Test Menu Overview

| Pathogen | Disease | Test | Sample |
|----------|---------|------|--------|
| CCV (coronavirus) | Mild enteritis | Antigen | Fecal |
| CAV-1 (adenovirus) | Infectious hepatitis | Antibody / PCR | Serum / blood |
| Leishmania | Leishmaniasis | Antibody | Serum |
| Toxoplasma | Toxoplasmosis | Antibody | Serum |

## Canine Coronavirus (CCV)

CCV causes mild-to-moderate enteritis, often co-infecting with CPV. Antigen testing is rapid but CCV is generally less severe than CPV.

| Note | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Clinical severity | Generally milder than CPV |
| Co-infection | Common with CPV — test both |
| Vaccination | Not always core; check protocol |

## Adenovirus (CAV-1)

CAV-1 causes infectious canine hepatitis. Detection is typically by antibody or PCR; antigen POCT is less common.

## Leishmania & Toxoplasma

Both are antibody-detected infections with regional (Leishmania) or zoonotic (Toxoplasma) significance.

| Infection | Clinical context |
|-----------|------------------|
| Leishmaniasis | Endemic in Mediterranean/Latin America; chronic, multisystemic |
| Toxoplasmosis | Zoonotic; clinical disease rare in adult dogs |

## FAQ

**Should I test for CCV alongside CPV?** In a puppy with acute gastroenteritis, yes — co-infection is common and CPV alone may not explain all signs.

**When is Leishmania testing indicated?** In endemic regions, or in imported dogs with compatible chronic signs (weight loss, lymphadenopathy, skin lesions).
