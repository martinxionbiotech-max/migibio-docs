# Feline Panleukopenia (FPV) Testing: Complete Guide

Feline panleukopenia virus (FPV) is a highly contagious parvovirus causing severe enteritis and panleukopenia in cats — especially kittens. It is closely related to canine parvovirus, and rapid antigen testing is the diagnostic mainstay.

## When to Test

| Signal | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Acute vomiting/diarrhea + fever (especially kitten) | Test immediately |
| Sudden death or illness in a cattery/litter | Test + outbreak protocol |
| Unexplained leukopenia | Test |
| Pre-intake screening (shelter) | Test per protocol |

## Detection: Antigen

FPV is detected via **antigen** (fecal), analogous to CPV testing. The virus sheds heavily in feces during acute infection.

## Result Interpretation

| Result | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| Antigen + clinical signs | Consistent with panleukopenia → isolate + supportive care |
| Antigen negative, high suspicion | Retest; shedding may be intermittent |
| Antigen positive, recently vaccinated | Possible transient vaccine-induced positive |

## Vaccination Interference

Modified-live FPV vaccines can produce transient antigen positivity. Interpret positives in recently vaccinated, clinically normal cats with caution.

## FPV vs CPV — Why It Matters

FPV and CPV are closely related parvoviruses. Some assays cross-react — confirm your assay's specificity, and note that FPV is the more severe feline pathogen.

## FAQ

**How fast is the result?** ~15 minutes on the Migibio platform.

**What is the prognosis for panleukopenia?** Guarded in kittens; early detection and aggressive supportive care improve survival.
